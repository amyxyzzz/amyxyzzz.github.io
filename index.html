Samwise, a 2U CubeSat

Stanford Space Initiative, satellites team co-lead. Sep 2024 to present.

I lead end-to-end design and delivery of Samwise, launching on SpaceX Transporter-18. It carries three cameras, UHF and S-band radios, full attitude control, and custom avionics across 13 unique PCBs.

I redesigned the primary RP2350B flight computer in KiCad, adding the burn-wire deployment and watchdog circuits that make antenna and panel release reliable. I brought up and tested every flight board, wrote the design documentation, and built 2.4 GHz radio test systems for image downlink with UART integration to the software team.

Mixed-signal RGB laser projector

Final project, June 2026.

Two custom boards that drive red, green and blue class 4 laser diodes, a galvanometer and a spinning polygon mirror from a 3.3 V RISC-V controller. The hard part was keeping noisy, high-current loads away from the logic.

The power and motion board generates six rails from a boost, four bucks and an LDO, with a charge-pump inverter for the negative supply. Step-down converters handle the heavy thermal dissipation of the linear laser drivers. The driver board sets each color's current with a DAC and op-amp loop, with separate signal and power connectors.

Through-wall 3D imaging radar

Final project for a course on 3D imaging sensors, May 2026. Best project in class.

The course covered how the 3D imaging sensors used across industry actually work: imaging physics, data acquisition, image formation, and the signal and image quality metrics that carry over from one sensor type to another.

For the final project, my group programmed a TI IWR6843 radar module and wrote the algorithms to detect people through a wall and count how many are moving, getting the count right most of the time. I built a performance model that predicts target SNR through different wall materials, and we tracked each person with TDM-MIMO range-Doppler processing and Kalman filtering.

Circuit–thruster coupling in Hall effect thruster tests

Plasma Dynamics Modeling Laboratory, with Prof. Kentaro Hara and NASA’s JANUS team. Apr 2025 to Jun 2026.

Ground vacuum chambers change how Hall effect thrusters behave: walls, harnesses and pressure all couple into the discharge. Because the discharge is nonlinear, standard impedance definitions break down.

I built an equivalent circuit of the facility, reconstructed in-chamber voltages from outside probes, and tested impedance definitions on toy problems. Effective resistance, the FFT of v/i, captured the thruster’s breathing mode where FFT(v)/FFT(i) failed. Presented at IEPC 2025, awarded best contribution of the session.
